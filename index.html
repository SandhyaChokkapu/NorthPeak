The route

Six camps, one summit push.

Every engagement climbs the same route, in the same order — each camp has to hold before the next one starts.

  1. Camp I

    Discovery

    Stakeholder interviews, audience research, and a hard look at what's actually working today.

  2. Camp II

    Strategy

    Positioning, messaging architecture, and a content plan tied to a measurable outcome.

  3. Camp III

    Design

    A visual system built for the brand, not borrowed from a template library.

  4. Camp IV

    Build

    Hand-coded, accessible front ends. No page builders, no bloat, no plugin roulette.

  5. Camp V

    Launch

    QA across devices, analytics wired up correctly, and a rollback plan in case weather turns.

  6. Camp VI

    Ascent

    Ongoing iteration: what the data says, what to test next, what to cut.

Before you ask

Questions we get on every call.

Why fixed price instead of hourly?

Because hourly billing rewards us for going slow. We scope the route up front, quote it once, and hold the number — the only way that works is if the scope is honest from the start.

What if we need something outside the original scope?

It happens on most projects. We quote the addition separately before touching it, so nothing shows up as a surprise on the final invoice.

Do you use templates or page builders?

No. Every site is hand-coded, which is slower on day one and faster, lighter, and easier to change for the next five years.

How fast can you actually turn things around?

Basecamp projects run two weeks from kickoff. Ridgeline runs five. Those timelines start once copy and brand assets are in our hands, not on signature day.

What happens after launch?

You own the code outright. Some clients walk away at that point; most move to Summit for ongoing iteration once real traffic starts telling us what to fix next.
